About Marvel

Marvel Entertainment, LLC, formerly known as Timely Publications and Atlas Comics, was founded in 1939. In the 1960s, it was renamed Marvel Comics and began publishing the stories of beloved characters such as Spider-Man, Iron Man, Thor, Hulk, Captain America, and the X-Men. This company has created the modern mythology.Its comics present superheroes’ amazing powers combined with human vulnerability.

Introduction:Spider-Man

Spider-Man is the quintessential everyman superhero in the comic book world. He made his debut in Marvel Comics' Amazing Fantasy, no. 15 back in 1962. The story follows Peter Parker, a teenager who’s not only an orphan but also struggling with health issues. His life takes a dramatic turn when he gets bitten by a radioactive spider, granting him incredible powers like superhuman strength, speed, and agility, plus the ability to stick to walls. Created by the talented duo of writer Stan Lee and illustrator Steve Ditko, Spider-Man was initially just a filler story for a canceled anthology series. At that time, having a teenage hero was a novel concept in comics. But young readers connected with Peter Parker in a big way, leading to an ongoing series and eventually a massive media franchise that includes video games, multiple animated shows, a live-action TV series, a blockbuster film series, and even a Broadway musical.

Story

Peter Parker was an ordinary teenager living in Queens, New York, with his Aunt May and Uncle Ben. One day, during a school field trip to a science exhibition, Peter was bitten by a radioactive spider. Soon after, he discovered that he had gained incredible strength, agility, the ability to climb walls, and a mysterious spider-sense that warned him of danger. Using his scientific knowledge, Peter also created web-shooters that allowed him to swing across the city. At first, Peter did not use his powers to help others. Instead, he used them for entertainment and personal gain. However, everything changed when a burglar he had previously allowed to escape killed Uncle Ben. Peter was devastated and blamed himself for what had happened. Remembering Uncle Ben's lesson, “With great power comes great responsibility,” Peter decided to use his abilities to protect innocent people. From that day on, Peter became Spider-Man. He began fighting criminals and protecting the people of New York while trying to maintain his normal life as a student. However, being a superhero was not easy. Peter had to deal with dangerous villains, personal losses, and the constant challenge of keeping his identity a secret. Despite these difficulties, he continued to fight for what was right, learning that being a hero was not only about having great powers, but also about making responsible choices and protecting others.

Sidekick Relationship
Miles Morales Miles Morales is the second Spider-Man to appear in Ultimate Marvel, an imprint with a separate continuity from the mainstream Marvel Universe called the Ultimate Universe (Earth-1610). He first appeared in Ultimate Fallout #4 (August 2011), following the death of the Ultimate Peter Parker. The character was initially introduced in the alternate Earth-1610 (portrayed in the Ultimate Universe comics), in which he was bitten by a spider genetically engineered at the behest of Norman Osborn in an attempt to duplicate the abilities of Spider-Man. He was featured in the Ultimate Comics: Spider-Man comic book series. After Marvel ended the Ultimate imprint in 2015, Miles was transported to the main Marvel Universe (Earth-616), beginning with stories under the All-New, All-Different Marvel branding that debuted that same year, depicted as the fourth Spider-Man of that universe (after Peter Parker, Ben Reilly, and Otto Octavius). Reaction to the character was mixed. Some, including Spider-Man's co-creator, Stan Lee, approved the creation of a positive role model for children of color. Others expressed displeasure at the replacement of Peter Parker, with The Guardian, Fox News, and Culture Map Houston reporting that some fans viewed the decision as an attempt by Marvel Comics to exhibit political correctness, and that the introduction of a minority Spider-Man was simply a publicity stunt to attract more readers, a charge Alonso denied. Alexandra Petri of The Washington Post called for the character to be judged on the quality of his stories, which garnered positive reviews
Spider-Gwen(Gwen Stacy) This vision of Gwen Stacy as a spider-powered heroine was first seen by long-time Spider-Man writer Dan Slott in a draft that he created for the "Spider-Verse" storyline in 2014 to 2015. Nonetheless, this concept is very far from the one that came out in print, and mostly the creation of the creators of Spider-Gwen, namely Jason Latour and Robbi Rodriguez. In October, Nick Lowe said at New York Comic Con 2014 that the beloved Spider-Gwen introduced in "Spider-Verse" would get its own series due to many demands from the fans. The first volume ended in the fifth issue, and the character then moved to the second volume of "Spider-Verse," as well as the story "Secret Wars." Right after the story finished, a second volume by the same creators started as an issue #1 of All-New, All-Different Marvel imprint.
Ganke Lee Ganke Lee is a Korean-American and best friend of Miles Morales and one of the very few people whom Miles confided about his abilities. It is Ganke who deduces that Miles has got powers identical to those of Spider-Man. He becomes aware of the power of Miles when he enters his room in a hurry and narrates the events that led to him gaining such powers. Enthused by the 'cool' powers of Miles and assuming that Miles was a mutant, Ganke advised Miles to become a superhero and make use of his abilities; while Miles, on the other hand, did not want to use his powers at all and wished for a normal life. Later, Ganke makes the right guess that the powers of Miles do not come from the X-Gene and are rather due to genetic mutations

Antihero of Spider-Man

Antihero Relationship
Venom Venom is perhaps the most appealing villain in this list of villains. He is mean, powerful and endowed with Spider-man’s powers in addition to other powers. Venom is a creature of teeth, muscles and insanity. He first appeared in 1988 with the release of Amazing Spider-Man #301. Before Amazing Spider-Man #300, the superhero Spider-Man had been fighting with his newly acquired black suit that had been made out of a symbiote alien that had fallen on a meteorite both physically and morally. The hero almost gave in to the suit but finally beat it. The symbiote alien then teamed up with a journalist by the name of Eddie Brock, who did not like Peter Parker. Again, the alien was able to dominate him and create a monster.
Morbius Doctor Michael Morbius was a fictional character that was conceived by Roy Thomas and Gil Kane. He was afflicted by a disease that could cut short his life span. He tried to cure himself using science, just as many mad scientists had done before him. He transformed himself into a vampire with strength, flight and healing powers.
Carnage Even though there came a point when the symbiote and Brock separated, the reign of terror of Venom continued. While Brock was imprisoned, the symbiote came back, and this time, there was something that happened differently. The symbiote split into smaller pieces, and one of the pieces fused with another host known as a murderer named Cletus Kasady and became Carnage. It was called Carnage because it represented chaos due to an evil alien bonding with a bad human being, and just like the name Venom that means hatred. Just like Venom, but much more vicious and stronger, is Carnage. Spider-Man and Venom have to team up in order to have a chance at beating the stronger symbiote. There are many comics where Carnage appeared including “Carnage: Family Feud” by Zeb Wells.”
